# 9/22/26

>In your journal write about how your work fits in with other work in a similar vein. How is your contribution unique? How does it build off of other’s work? 

When I think of games that involve grief, the one that comes to mind first is Clair Obscur: Expedition 33.  Just in the prologue the player is confronted with it.  Many players say how powerful that scene is and that even on subsequent playthroughs it still affects them.  

When I started approaching my thesis project I thought of what made that prologue scene and other scenes in the game so gut wrenching.  I came up with a few answers including things like the music and becoming quickly attached to characters.  

But what I believe to be one of the biggest influences is how real that sense of grief and loss is presented in the game.  Most people have experienced it at some point in their lives.  We understand losing someone.  We understand that grief can be from other sources like loss of time or being unable to do something you always wanted to do.  The scene at the harbor shows both of these sources.  And as you see not only people but potential fading away, you empathize and mourn for them.  I want to explore Clair Obscur's use of storytelling and raw depiction of grief and loss as an inspiration for the narrative elements of my game.  

My game is unique not just in subject matter, but also in perspective.  While Avery will represent a typical picture of loss and grief, Gwen will represent a more nuanced one.  Throughout the story Gwen will come to terms that they can't help their best friend anymore.  I want to create a game that uses narrative and interactivity to showcase these perspectives.

# 9/29/26

> Write about your plans for the midterm demo day. What will you be ready to show. What challenges will you face in the next 4 weeks to make this work? What state do you expect your project to be in, and how do you anticipate it will be received by the faculty critics?

My goal for demo day is to have all the game mechanics complete.  I want the navigation between rooms in the apartment to be tested and complete and the inventory system to be tested and complete.  If there’s time, I’d also like to make a map or guide to make tracking what rooms have been visited easily visible to the player.

This will mean that most likely the Twine file I’ll use will be separate from the actual game file.  The demo file will jump directly into the interactive part of the game so that the audience doesn’t have to read through any story to see how the game works.

A whole pillar of my research is around narrative and storytelling.  I expect to spend a lot of time learning about how storytelling works in games and how it differs from storytelling in non-interactive media such as books.  I don’t want to set a goal of having a complete script by demo day.  I will present briefly on what my plans are in terms of character perspectives, unreliable narration, and the ways Avery and Gwen portray grief.

The challenges will be making decisions about the game mechanics and design without having time to user test it, explain my storytelling ideas succinctly while still making it sound compelling, and potentially creating the map.

My hopes are that my project is received well and I get actionable feedback.  I anticipate that most feedback will be around my visual design since currently I just have a box around some text on a black background.  Beyond that I would like to get feedback around my storytelling ideas.
